This summer, the 7th Annual Back 2 School Tour brought barrier-free back-to-school resources to 14,600+ community members — West End Atlanta, Little Haiti, Miami, South Central LA, Exposition Park and Venice Beach, and the Bronx.
No ID required. No barriers. No cost.

Every event was shaped by the community it served. 190+ partner organizations showed up. 35+ elected offices engaged. And 350+ volunteers didn't just distribute backpacks — they cleaned beaches, staffed tables, and walked families to the resources they needed.

This is what community infrastructure looks like when you build it together.

 

The tour opened in Atlanta's West End with Baby2Baby, The Jones Project, and NextEra Energy. Families went home with backpacks, diapers, wipes, electric toothbrushes, and hygiene essentials. A first stop in a city IBTU came to build with, not visit.

 

Year three in Little Haiti, built with Baby2Baby, Florida Power & Light, Commissioner Christine King, and Haitian community organizations, with a Haitian-American host on the mic. Backpacks, diapers, LEGO sets, electric toothbrushes, wipes, and deodorant went home with families. Grocery gift cards were drawn from the stage every half hour, because families decide what their household needs.

 

Seven straight years in South LA and families still lined up long before the doors opened. Fully loaded backpacks, diapers and wipes, free haircuts, smoothies, and four youth activations ran all day, with LA Care Health Plan, ONE Church and United MegaCare among the partners on the floor. Asked what they needed most, one household wrote: "Community. Compassion. Connecting with others in LA so we don't feel so alone."

 

Every Alliance campus, from Watts to Sun Valley, on one lawn for the first time. IBTU designed, built, and ran the network's first Back 2 School Resource Fair end to end. Backpacks with supply kits, books, diapers, SmartyPants vitamins, Sol de Janeiro hygiene products, clothes, shoes, and pallets of food went home at no cost to Alliance families. Elected officials spoke from the main stage and provided certificates to Alliance & IBTU.
Produced on behalf of Alliance College-Ready Public Schools.

 

Venice Beach came back for a second year, and Windward Plaza filled before the doors opened. Families moved through backpacks, free haircuts, food, and dental and vision screenings, with partners from Cedars-Sinai, CD11, Lindsey Horvath, The Marathan Burger to Westside Food Bank on the floor. Junebug and MyGuyMars ran a main stage that never paused. Households still rebuilding after the Eaton and Palisades fires had their own Fire Relief Zone, and two of them drove home in a Tesla for the weekend.

 

The tour closed in the Bronx with a Welcome Back BBQ at the Iovine & Young Center, built with New York Edge Community Schools, The Arthur Project, Knowlej, Astor Services, and Montefiore School Health. Backpacks with school supply kits, diapers, wipes, electric toothbrushes, body wash, and deodorant went home with families through Baby2Baby.

 

To the sponsors, partners and community organizations behind the 7th Annual Back 2 School Tour: 14,600+ community members walked in and left with what they came for because you said yes first. You underwrote whole stops, staffed tables in August heat, and sent trucks before we finished asking. Thank you for building with us.
